Replace a lost socket with a second set, not a single

A 120-piece socket set at $89 costs 74 cents per piece, and the pieces you actually turn are the ones worth counting. Turn ten different sizes a year and the set works out to $8.90 per used piece. The other 110 sockets are not dead weight; they are the reason the next repair starts instead of stopping at the store.

The second set is the cheapest missing-size policy on the shelf. Lose a 10mm and the $4 duplicate kit puts it back in the drawer, ten times the per-piece price for the piece that matters. In practice the two sets share the drawer and the ten sizes you turn live in both, so the backup of every daily size costs about $4 a year.

Single replacements carry a floor. A $8 socket plus $7 shipping is a $15 fill, so six fills cost $90 and pass the whole set price on the sixth. The crossover sits just under six fills. One lost 10mm a year is cheaper to fill loose; two losses in a year and the second set wins outright, before counting the afternoon the store had to order it.
